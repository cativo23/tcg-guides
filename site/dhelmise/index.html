How it takes Prizes

The deck's plan in four steps: the discard pile is the real board.

  1. 1

    Discard

    Gwynn discards 2 Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon straight from hand and draws 6.

  2. 2

    Dig

    Explorer's Guidance, Poké Pad and Ultra Ball keep finding more Hide 'n' Sneak bodies and spending them.

  3. 3

    Stack

    Telepathic Psychic Energy benches 2 more Basic Psychic Pokémon on attach; Prism Tower trades 2 cards for 1.

  4. 4

    170 / wipe

    4 in the discard: Vengeful Anchor hits for 170. 6 in the discard: Matcha Spin drops 4 counters on everything they have.

How it plays

Opening

  • Keep any hand with a Basic. Best: Poltchageist (Retreat 0) plus Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Ultra Ball or Poké Pad.
  • What finds what: Poké Pad gets anything without a Rule Box. Ultra Ball gets anything, Lillie's Clefairy ex included, for a discard-2 cost. Buddy-Buddy Poffin benches two 70-HP-or-less Basics: Shuppet, Poltchageist, Dunsparce.
  • Start Poltchageist or Shuppet Active, not Dhelmise. Dhelmise is Retreat 3 and weak to Darkness; keep it Benched until the discard count is actually ready, or until Latias ex's Skyliner is in play to retreat it for free.
  • Going second with 2+ spare Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon already in hand: Gwynn immediately for 6 cards. Otherwise Explorer's Guidance or Lillie's Determination.
  • Bench order (5 slots): 2–3 Hide 'n' Sneak Basics → Dunsparce → Dhelmise once it's time to attack.

The engine

  • Discard, don't play out, the Hide 'n' Sneak line. Every Shuppet, Banette, Poltchageist or Sinistcha that reaches the discard pile — by Gwynn, Explorer's Guidance, Ultra Ball's own cost, or a Knock Out — counts toward both thresholds.
  • Only one Supporter a turn, and this list runs four: Gwynn, Explorer's Guidance, Boss's Orders, Lillie's Determination. Gwynn when the hand already holds 2 to discard; Explorer's Guidance when it doesn't.
  • Telepathic Psychic Energy, attached to any Psychic Pokémon in play, searches up to 2 more Basic Psychic Pokémon onto the Bench the same turn — the fastest way to a second Dhelmise without drawing it.
  • Prism Tower: play it only after Gwynn or Explorer's Guidance have already thinned the hand. It lets the opponent filter for free too.
  • Latias ex's Skyliner Ability gives every Basic Pokémon in play free retreat — once it's on the Bench, Dhelmise's own Retreat 3 stops being a problem, and so does Poltchageist/Shuppet/Dunsparce shuffling in and out.

Winning

  • Vengeful Anchor is 30 base, 170 once 4 Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon are discarded. This session's simulation: maxing Gwynn and Explorer's Guidance to 4 copies each, with 4 Sinistcha in the discard-fodder count, clearly beats every lower count tried at reaching that 4th discard quickly — treat the ranking as real, not the exact percentage, since two separate simulation runs gave different absolute numbers for the same build.
  • Matcha Spin needs 6 in the discard pile and puts 4 counters on every opposing Pokémon at once — the deck's answer to a wide Bench, not a single-target finisher.
  • Boss's Orders pulls up whatever Matcha Spin's spread already hurt, or a Basic before it evolves.
  • Dudunsparce is a free reload, not a Prize risk: Run Away Draw draws 3 and shuffles it and everything attached back into the deck, so the opponent never banks a Knock Out on it. Only 1 copy runs in this build — Dunsparce itself survives the April 2027 rotation but Dudunsparce doesn't, so don't expect to replace it with a newer printing afterward.
  • Bloodmoon Ursaluna ex's Blood Moon is the deck's single biggest hit (240, any 5 Energy) — hold it for a Knock Out it can actually land, since it can't attack again the following turn.

Decisions

  • Gwynn or Explorer's Guidance: Gwynn when the hand already holds 2 Pokémon worth discarding — immediate 6 cards, no filtering. Explorer's Guidance when it doesn't; it looks at 6 and keeps the best 2, usually discarding at least one more Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon from the rest.
  • Don't let Explorer's Guidance or Ultra Ball discard the single Legacy Energy, Pokégear 3.0, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Latias ex or Bloodmoon Ursaluna ex — only 1 copy of each runs here, and the last two are 2-Prize cards you actually want to play, not feed to the discard pile like the Hide 'n' Sneak line.
  • Night Stretcher: this deck wants its Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon to stay discarded. Use it to get back a basic Energy or a specific Pokémon you're actually short of in play, not reflexively.
  • Lillie's Clefairy ex: bench it for Fairy Zone against a Darkness attacker, since it turns their Weakness to Psychic. It's 190 HP but weak to Metal and worth 2 Prizes; don't leave it Active without a reason.

Combos

  • Telepathic Psychic Energy + an open Bench slot: search 2 more Basic Psychic Pokémon onto the Bench the same turn Gwynn discards 2 more — up to 4 Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon move in one turn.
  • Gwynn into Explorer's Guidance across two turns: Gwynn clears 2 immediately, then Explorer's Guidance usually finds at least 1 more from the next top 6.
  • Ultra Ball's own discard-2 cost: pay it with spare Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon instead of Energy, turning a search Item into free discard-pile progress.
  • Matcha Spin + Boss's Orders: once 6 are discarded, Matcha Spin's 4 counters land on the whole board; Boss's Orders the next turn finishes whatever it didn't already Knock Out.

Misplays

  • Attacking with a Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon instead of discarding it. Puppet Pull (80) and Hang Down (10) are real attacks, but this deck's value is in the discard pile, not the Active Spot.
  • Using Night Stretcher to bring a Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon back "to be safe." That undoes progress toward the 4/6 threshold.
  • Playing Prism Tower before Gwynn or Explorer's Guidance have already filled the discard pile — it hands the opponent the same filter for free.
  • Paying to retreat Dudunsparce. Its own Retreat is 3, but Run Away Draw already returns it and everything attached to the deck for free.
  • Benching Lillie's Clefairy ex out of habit. It's a 2-Prize, Metal-weak target; only bench it when Fairy Zone is actually relevant.

Hide 'n' Sneak blocks effects of attacks and Abilities aimed at that Pokémon specifically — its own card text says "Damage is not an effect." So Matcha Spin's counters, and an opponent's own spread damage, still land on a Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon; only secondary effects (Special Conditions, extra effects tacked onto an attack) are stopped.
